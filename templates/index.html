EXPLANATION

Why Linearise?

In science and engineering, data rarely follow a straight line. Two ubiquitous non-linear models are the exponential ($y = Ae^{bx}$) and the power law ($y = Ax^b$). Applying a logarithmic transformation converts each into a linear form, making the unique closed-form OLS solution directly applicable — no iterative optimisation required.

◈
Core Principle Any model writable as $\ln y = c + bX$ (for some transform $X$) is linearisable. OLS recovers $b$ and $c$; the prefactor follows by back-transformation as $A = e^c$.

1 · Exponential Model: $y = Ae^{bx}$

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides collapses the model to a straight line:

$$\ln y = \underbrace{\ln A}_{c} + bx \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad Y = bx + c$$

With $Y_i = \ln y_i$ and $c = \ln A$, the OLS normal equations on the pairs $(x_i, Y_i)$ give:

$$b = \frac{n\sum x_i Y_i - \sum x_i \sum Y_i}{n\sum x_i^2 - \bigl(\sum x_i\bigr)^2} \qquad c = \bar{Y} - b\bar{x} \qquad A = e^c$$
SymbolMeaningRole in model
$A$Prefactor / amplitudeValue of $y$ when $x = 0$
$b$Rate constant$b > 0$ growth, $b < 0$ decay
$Y_i$$\ln y_i$Linearised response variable
$c$$\ln A$OLS intercept in log-space
ℹ
Typical applications Bacterial colony growth, radioactive decay, capacitor discharge, compound interest — anywhere a quantity changes by a constant percentage per unit time.

2 · Power-Law Model: $y = Ax^b$

Taking logarithms of both $x$ and $y$ linearises the power law on a log–log scale:

$$\ln y = \underbrace{\ln A}_{c} + b\ln x \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad Y = bX + c$$

Setting $Y_i = \ln y_i$, $X_i = \ln x_i$, and $c = \ln A$, the same OLS formulae apply:

$$b = \frac{n\sum X_i Y_i - \sum X_i \sum Y_i}{n\sum X_i^2 - \bigl(\sum X_i\bigr)^2} \qquad c = \bar{Y} - b\bar{X} \qquad A = e^c$$
ℹ
Typical applications Fluid drag ($F \propto v^2$), allometry, Kepler's Third Law, Zipf's Law, power-grid load scaling — wherever a log–log plot appears linear.

3 · Goodness of Fit: $R^2$

Always evaluate $R^2$ in the original (not log-transformed) space:

$$R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_\text{res}}{SS_\text{tot}} = 1 - \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_i - \bar{y})^2}$$
RangeInterpretation
$R^2 = 1.000$Perfect — model passes through every data point
$\ge 0.990$Excellent — typical for clean physical measurements
$0.90$–$0.99$Good — may show systematic residual pattern
$< 0.90$Poor — consider an alternative model family
$< 0$Model is worse than simply predicting $\bar{y}$
⚑
Critical caveat Computing $R^2$ in log-space systematically overstates fit quality because the logarithm compresses large values. Always back-transform $\hat{y}_i = Ae^{b x_i}$ (or $Ax_i^b$) and compare to the raw $y_i$.

4 · Assumptions & Limitations

Requirements
  • All $y_i > 0$ — both models require taking $\ln y$
  • All $x_i > 0$ — power law additionally requires $\ln x$
  • At least $n \ge 3$ distinct data points
  • Sufficient spread in $x$ to resolve the curvature
Inherent Limitations
  • OLS in log-space minimises relative, not absolute, errors
  • Small $y$ values gain disproportionate weight after log-transform
  • Outliers in log-space can be catastrophic in original scale
  • Confidence intervals require careful back-transformation

5 · Step-by-Step Procedure

Validate inputs. Confirm all $y_i > 0$. For power-law, also confirm all $x_i > 0$. Require $n \ge 3$. Reject any zero or negative values before proceeding.

Apply the logarithm. Compute $Y_i = \ln y_i$ for all $i$. For power-law additionally compute $X_i = \ln x_i$. For exponential, $X_i = x_i$ (unchanged).

Accumulate the five sums. Compute $n$, $\sum X_i$, $\sum Y_i$, $\sum X_i^2$, and $\sum X_i Y_i$. These five quantities contain all the information OLS needs.

Solve the normal equations. Calculate the slope $b$ from the five sums, then the intercept $c = \bar{Y} - b\bar{X}$. Check the denominator $D = n\sum X^2 - (\sum X)^2 \ne 0$.

Back-transform. Recover $A = e^c$. The exponent $b$ carries over unchanged. The fitted model is now fully defined.

Compute $R^2$ in original space. Evaluate $\hat{y}_i$ for each $i$, compute $SS_\text{res}$ and $SS_\text{tot}$, then $R^2 = 1 - SS_\text{res}/SS_\text{tot}$.

Inspect the residual plot. Plot $e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$ against $x_i$. A random scatter around zero confirms the model is appropriate; systematic curvature or fan-shapes signal model misfit.

Formula Sheet

Exponential: $y = Ae^{bx}$

$$Y_i=\ln y_i,\quad X_i=x_i\text{ (unchanged)}$$ $$b=\frac{n\sum xY-\sum x\sum Y}{n\sum x^2-(\sum x)^2}$$ $$c=\bar{Y}-b\bar{x}\qquad A=e^c$$

Power Law: $y = Ax^b$

$$Y_i=\ln y_i,\quad X_i=\ln x_i$$ $$b=\frac{n\sum XY-\sum X\sum Y}{n\sum X^2-(\sum X)^2}$$ $$c=\bar{Y}-b\bar{X}\qquad A=e^c$$

Goodness of Fit

$$R^2=1-\frac{\sum(y_i-\hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum(y_i-\bar{y})^2}$$

Compute in original space — not log-space

Derived Time Constants

$$t_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{|b|}\quad(\text{decay},\;b<0)$$ $$T_d=\frac{\ln2}{b}\quad(\text{growth},\;b>0)$$
◈
Key distinction Exponential vs Power-Law differ in only one step: for exponential keep $X_i = x_i$; for power-law set $X_i = \ln x_i$. Every other formula is identical.

Terminology

Glossary

Linearisation
Transforming a non-linear model ($e^{bx}$ or $x^b$) into a linear form so that OLS can be applied analytically.
OLS (Ordinary Least Squares)
Minimises the sum of squared vertical residuals; yields a unique closed-form solution for slope and intercept.
Prefactor (A)
Back-transformed intercept $A = e^c$. For the exponential model, equals $y$ at $x = 0$.
Rate Constant (b)
Controls growth ($b > 0$) or decay ($b < 0$) per unit $x$ in the exponential model $y = Ae^{bx}$.
Scaling Exponent (b)
Log–log slope in power-law $y = Ax^b$. $b=1$ linear, $b=2$ quadratic, $0{<}b{<}1$ sub-linear.
R² (Coefficient of Determination)
Fraction of variance in $y$ explained by the model. Always computed in original (not log) space.
SS_res
Residual sum of squares: $\sum(y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2$. Measures unexplained variation.
SS_tot
Total sum of squares: $\sum(y_i - \bar{y})^2$. Total variation in the response variable.
Half-Life
For exponential decay: $t_{1/2} = \ln 2 / |b|$. The time for the response to halve in value.
Doubling Time
For exponential growth: $T_d = \ln 2 / b$. The time for the response to double.
Residual ($e_i$)
$e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$. Positive = under-prediction; negative = over-prediction by the model.
Back-Transformation
Converting the OLS log-space intercept back: $A = e^c$, while slope $b$ is unchanged.

Decision Guide

Exponential vs Power-Law

Exponential $y = Ae^{bx}$
  • Constant percentage rate of change
  • Semi-log plot ($\ln y$ vs $x$) is linear
  • $x$ may be zero or negative
  • Only $y$ is log-transformed; $X_i = x_i$
  • Time, voltage, temperature as predictor
Power Law $y = Ax^b$
  • Log–log plot ($\ln y$ vs $\ln x$) is linear
  • Requires all $x > 0$
  • Both $x$ and $y$ are log-transformed
  • Exponent $b$ has direct physical meaning
  • Relationship follows a scaling law
⚑
Diagnostic plots first Always inspect both the semi-log and log–log plots before selecting a model. A high $R^2$ is necessary but not sufficient — always check the residual pattern too.
